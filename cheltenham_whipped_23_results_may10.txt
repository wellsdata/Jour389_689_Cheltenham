BOYS CLUBBED IN CHELTENHAM SAY INMATES Former Inmates Writes Afro- American Of
Barbarities Practiced At Reform School PRISON METHOD USED Solitary Cells, Bread
And Water Diet, Made Skeletons Of Boys, Is Charge That the worst form of
crulety and shameful clubbinig of boys with clubs were practiced at the House
of Re- formation at Cheltenham, is the charge made by William M. Creasy, for
more than three years an inmate of the institution, to this paper this week.
Creasy, who volunteered this infor- Is mation, offers to come here as a wit-
ness and testify as to detall happen- dings which took place while he was
there. His letter follows: Editor AFRO-AMERICAN: I was reading last week's
AFRO, and I noticed your Cheltenham ar- ticle. I was an inmate of the House of
Reformation in 1912, and I can frankly say that every word in your article is
true. The only thing you have not found out half that goes on on the inside of
the place. Bread and tea is certainly the chief diet, and not much of that. Two
slices of bread and half a cup of tea to a person at breakfast and supper. At
dinner the same bread and tea with a bowl of worm broth which they called soup.
Work Without Food When the officers are angry at something they cut the boys
out of three and four meals hand running and make them work just the same. They
have big straps there which they beat the boys with, and they have handles 2½
feet long and the Ilmber part which boys are hit with is just a little longer.
The farm overseer is a big colored man weighing 225 or 250 pounds, and he is
the chief beater. Mr. J. B. Pyles, the superintendent, used to give his
overseer, whose name is Harry Washington, a quart of whis- key and the strap,
and sometimes they would go to all five of the schools and beat all the boys
un- mercifully. Dies After Blow Whispering or talking is strictly against the
rules, except to an of- ficer or guard. While I was there one boy, John Baker,
received a blow over the head with a window stick, and he died a few weeks
after getting the blow. E. D. Furgang, the teacher at that time of No. 4
school, was the man who delivered the blow. I was beaten nearly to death be-
cause I sobbed too loud when I re- ceived a letter that my mother was.. dead.
This was about the third month that I was there. Prison Methods Used When a boy
would run away he was confined in a small cell, 8 x 3, from 3 to 6 months. His
diet was two thin clices of bread, two cups of water a day, the first thirty
days he was confined. The superinten- dent would bring Washington around every
day, and the boy who was in confinement would receive a severe beating every
day for the first thirty days. By this time these boys would look like walking
skeletons. Two School Days In Three Years I was at Cheltenham 3 years, 5
months, and a few days, and I had ' school two afternoons during this time.
They would hire the boys out with mean people who hated colored peo- ple and
the boys were worked just 3 like slaves: They gave us a fairly good sized piece
of meat each Thanksgiving, and this the only time we did get any meat. I was
sent to, Cheltenham from this town and the men who sent me are still here. If
they are making an investigation of the House of Reformation and need a witness
who realy knows the inside of the place I am the man. Very truly yours, WM. M.
CREASY.

Filth And Brutality At Cheltenham The latest report of filth and brutality at
the Cheltenham School for Colored Boys follows a pattern that has been going on
for twenty years. Cheltenham has long been one of those civic disgraces that
everyone talks about and no one does anything about. Now Thomas B. Sprague, the
current president of the board, has ordered a thorough investiga. tion of
reports that the boys are being whipped and abused. It is to be hoped that he
can do something to improve conditions in that medieval rattrap that Maryland
so graciously calls a reform school for young Nezroes. Mr. Sprague has been
told by the inmates that whipping with a strap and a cat-o-nine- tails is
common practice at Cheltenham. Mr. Sprague himself, on a surprise visit to the
institution, found a large leather lash in a supervisor's drawer, Mr. Sprague
also found that three of the five cottages were "inexcusably filthy." Worst of
all, he found & young boy locked and unattended in a wire storage cage who was
just recovering from an operation and was still running a fever. Such abuses
Mr. Sprague should be able to correct. There is no excuse for brutality or
filth. At the same time, the State should not entertain the notion that the
expenditure of large sums of money will do much to Improve the situation at
Cheltenham. The school is hopelessly outmoded, overcrowded and useless. Nothing
except a new school will cure the basic things wrong with Cheltenham. And the
funds for a new school were set aside by Governor O'Conor as a part of
Maryland's postwar construction pro- gram. Governor Lane wiped out the funds
for a new Cheltenham, and it is now up to Governor Lane to see to it that those
funds are restored. . .
